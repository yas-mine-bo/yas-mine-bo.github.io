About

Yasmine Boudiaf

Yasmine Boudiaf is a researcher, artist, and technologist based in London. She is a Research Fellow at UAL's Creative Computing Institute and a PhD candidate working at the intersection of generative AI, intangible cultural heritage, and decolonial practice. She was named one of 100 Brilliant Women in AI Ethics 2022, is a Visiting Fellow of the Ada Lovelace Institute, and a Fellow of the Royal Society of Arts.

Her practice spans participatory workshops, diagramming methodologies, and community-grounded approaches to AI and cultural heritage, with a particular focus on North African heritage traditions and decolonial frameworks. She describes her art practice as 'tin foil hat research' — playful projects rooted in deep research methodologies ranging from writing to computing, examining the shape-shifting tactics of ruling powers and new technologies.

As a serial founder, she built and directed Justice Matrix (2020–2024), a consultancy supporting non-profit and public-sector organisations in developing sustainable, socially responsible technology projects. Prior to that, she founded Serious Datum (2014–2019), specialising in ESG policy, change management, and values-based philanthropy for commercial clients, and served as Chief Strategist at Peoples Studio (2019–2022).

She has designed and led research at universities across the UK and internationally, including the University of Warwick, London South Bank University, Linnaeus University (Sweden), and the University of the Arts London. She is an Associate Researcher at Southbank University and a co-organiser of No Tech For Tyrants.
